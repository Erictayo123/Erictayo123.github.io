// 01  about

About

I'm currently studying Electrical Engineering, with a growing focus on embedded systems — the layer where code meets electricity. Most of my work lives somewhere between a microcontroller and a machine learning model: getting a neural network small enough, and a circuit reliable enough, that the two can actually work together.

My interests span TinyML, computer architecture, RISC-V, FPGAs, and digital design. I don't come from a background where any of this was easy — I was weak in math and physics early on, and most of what I know now came from building things until they worked, then figuring out why. That's still how I learn.

Outside of coursework, I help maintain a small community WiFi network, tinker with reverse engineering and CTF challenges, and try to keep a disciplined routine — early mornings, consistent training, steady progress. Long term, I'm aiming toward graduate study and a career built around hardware that thinks for itself.

Projects

TinyDrowsy ESP32

A lightweight CNN for driver drowsiness detection, running eye-state classification directly on an ESP32-CAM. Roughly 10K parameters, under 50 KB as an ONNX model, trained from scratch and quantized for on-device inference.

ESP32 AI Drowsiness Detection

The applied side of TinyDrowsy: dataset engineering from public and self-captured images, on-device deployment, and field testing under real lighting conditions — including what happens when the training data doesn't match the camera.
